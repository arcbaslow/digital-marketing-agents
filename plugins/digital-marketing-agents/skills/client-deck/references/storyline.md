# Storyline and reader architecture

## Write the answer before the slides

Use a read-ahead deck: a reader must understand the decision without hearing a talk. Start with the business situation, the material change or problem, and the answer. Write a titles-only storyboard before choosing layouts. Read it aloud as connected sentences; if a link is missing, repair the reasoning before adding graphics.

Use title, executive summary on slide 2, evidence sections, "what we need from you", appendix index and backup slides. A monthly report fits in about 8-12 main slides plus appendix, because the reader needs the change since last month, not the whole history. Keep recurring monthly sections in a stable order so readers can compare periods. The bilingual assets give separate report, audit and proposal spines.

## Executive summary

Answer what happened, why and what we ask the client to decide. Order summary points like the later evidence slides. State the material miss or uncertainty on this slide; forwarding only the summary must not create a false picture. Give the key ask an owner and date. Distinguish a forecast, target and actual result in the wording.

For an audit, lead with the most consequential verified gap and what decision it blocks. For a proposal, lead with the recommended scope, expected outcome, commercial basis and approval needed. Do not imply a proposal's estimates are achieved results.

## Action titles and evidence

Write each evidence title as a claim: subject, active verb, number and comparison when supported. Aim under fifteen words and at most two lines. "Channel performance" is a topic; "<Channel> reduced cost per sale by <change> in <period>" is a claim. If two claims compete, split the slide. Covers and section dividers orient the reader and need not manufacture a statistic.

The body proves the title. Use the simplest evidence form and two or three short lines explaining its business implication. Keep definitions needed for interpretation visible. Put exact lookup numbers in a table or appendix. Do not compress a full analysis onto one slide or make the body font smaller to avoid editing.

## Reconciliation before layout

Build a figure register with slide ID, metric, value, source sheet/cell or export filter, period, unit, definition, calculation and verification status. Recompute derived totals and changes from source values. Check net/gross spend, time zone, attribution, lead/sale status, reversals and rounding. Fix discrepancies with the source owner or mark them unresolved. Never choose whichever source supports the stronger story.

Use one resolved figure wherever it recurs. Reconcile before building and compare the rendered slide back to the register afterward. A past monthly reconciliation found four wrong figures in a finished deck; polish does not replace this check.

## Bad news and uncertainty

Put the miss in its title and the executive summary. Show the result against target or base, with absolute and relative change and both periods. For rates show percentage-point change alongside the underlying rates. A relative improvement can coexist with a decline in volume; report both if material to the decision.

For each miss state the size and period, confirmed cause, suspected cause or unknown, corrective action with owner/date, and the decision needed. Avoid apology openers and vague "headwinds". Write not done as not done and identify the blocker. Do not declare implementation from configuration alone.

Label preliminary or unverified figures on the slide, including a source discrepancy when material. Mark forecasts as forecasts. If there is insufficient evidence for a trend, say "not enough data" and show counts or a table with the limitation. A useful caveat cannot live only in notes.

## Asks, appendix and speaker notes

Use one to three concrete asks when possible, each with decision or action, owner, due date and impact of delay. Repeat the essential ones in the executive summary. Replace a generic thank-you ending with this next step. An audit asks for remediation ownership; a proposal asks for scope and funding approval; a report asks for the next operating decision.

Put methods, complete tables, definition changes and detailed evidence in an indexed appendix. Speaker notes hold the two likeliest objections, a concise answer to each, backup slide reference, source caveats and optional timing/transition. Do not put client-critical caveats or unapproved personal observations there. Internal objection notes belong in the working copy; make an intentional client-facing copy for handoff.
